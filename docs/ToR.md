# SECTION 4: TERMS OF REFERENCE

**Third-Party Monitoring of Six UNRWA Projects / Emergency Intervention Supported by the Government of Japan**

## Background

Established in 1949 under General Assembly Resolution 302 (IV), the United Nations Relief and Works Agency for Palestine Refugees in the Near East (UNRWA) stands out as one of the largest UN agencies, noted for its direct service delivery model. The Agency is tasked with supporting Palestine refugees registered in its six fields of operation, including Jordan, Lebanon, Syria, the Gaza Strip and the West Bank, including East Jerusalem. To this end, UNRWA offers a broad spectrum of services, including education, healthcare, relief and social services, infrastructure and camp improvement, microfinance, protection and emergency assistance.

In January 2026, the Government of Japan (GoJ) allocated US$ 34,333,332 from its supplementary budget to UNRWA. This support will provide essential services to Palestine refugees across multiple sectors under a range of projects and emergency interventions listed in the below table. Focusing on emergency assistance, healthcare, education and protection services, these project and emergency interventions will be operative during the period, 31 March 2026 through 30 March 2027 and will be governed by a dedicated Japan-UNRWA Project Management and Monitoring Mechanism (hereinafter referred to as the “Mechanism” elaborated under Annex 1), established in collaboration with the GoJ. In relation to the Mechanism, UNRWA is to contract a Third-Party Monitoring (TPM) service provider.

## Objective of TPM

UNRWA is dedicated to implementing and monitoring each of the above listed GoJ-supported projects / emergency interventions and the ongoing projects under no-cost extensions/reprogramming. Implementation will be guided by using indicators specified in the logical frameworks governing the project / emergency interventions. Existing humanitarian appeal, flash appeal and programme and project management and monitoring mechanisms will guide the implementation of related interventions. At present, UNRWA seeks to engage a service provider to design and implement independent TPM in relation to the above listed projects / emergency interventions as well as the ongoing projects under no-cost extensions/reprogramming. In this regard, the proposed TPM would:

* + 1. verify that projects / emergency interventions are implemented as per the approved project / intervention documents referenced through / embedded in the donor agreement;
    2. assess beneficiary satisfaction with the assistance provided (to provide the required level of assurance that project / emergency intervention goals are achieved); and
    3. ensure that resources are used effectively and in a transparent and traceable manner.

TPM arrangements should be viewed as both exceptional in nature and supplemental to the existing UNRWA programmes, emergency / flash appeals and project monitoring, reporting and evaluation processes, not as compliance, verification or audit exercise.

## Third Party Monitoring Management

* 1. **TPM Implementation phases**

The TPM service provider will carry out the following activities (as required, at the project / emergency intervention mid-way point and during the final month of project / emergency intervention implementation) to ensure transparency, traceability and effectiveness in the management of the above referenced six proposed projects / emergency interventions as well as the ongoing projects under no-cost extensions/reprogramming funded by the GoJ:

* **Project / emergency intervention document review**: Examine project / emergency intervention related documents, including project / emergency intervention proposals, workplans, budgets and other key documents.

* **Monitoring plan development**: Develop a comprehensive TPM monitoring plan, including relevant sampling methods and data collection tools.
* **Data collection**: Collect data on project / emergency intervention activities and outputs through interviews with key stakeholders and other relevant methods.
* **Site visits/monitoring**: Security and operational conditions permitting, conduct project / emergency intervention site visits to UNRWA facilities and/or refugee camps in Jordan, Lebanon and the West Bank, where and when the situation permits. The expected duration of each site visit will be between two and four working days. This cycle should be repeated during subsequent rounds of monitoring. For Gaza and Syria, remote monitoring may be conducted from Jordan or elsewhere.
* **Engagement with project / emergency intervention stakeholders and key actors**: Actively engage with key stakeholders, including direct interactions with UNRWA staff/personnel and beneficiaries to understand and document project / emergency intervention impacts and challenges.
* **Develop project / emergency intervention monitoring reports**: Generate a detailed interim monitoring report (one report that covers all six projects / emergency interventions and the ongoing projects under no-cost extensions/reprogramming) and one final monitoring report, supported by documents such as cleaned data sheets and photographs. These reports will: (i) verify the degree to which project / emergency interventions are being / have been implemented, as per the approved project / emergency intervention document / donor agreement; (ii) assess beneficiary satisfaction with the assistance provided (to provide the required level of assurance that project / emergency intervention goals are being achieved); and (iii) ensure that resources are used efficiently and in a traceable and transparent manner. The TPM contractor shall submit a draft version of the: (i) interim monitoring report (referenced in the deliverables and timelines section, below) to UNRWA for review and fact-checking no later than [eighteen (18) days] prior to the due date of the report; and (ii) final monitoring report, (also referenced in the deliverables and timelines section, below) to UNRWA for review and fact-checking no later than [eighteen (18) days] prior to the final report. On receipt of draft reports, UNRWA shall review to ensure accuracy, completeness and adherence to the agreed-upon terms of reference. UNRWA may provide comments, corrections and requests for additional information or clarification. The TPM service provider will incorporate all factually verified feedback and corrections requested by UNRWA to be incorporated in the final monitoring report. Revised versions of the draft reports, addressing UNRWA’s comments, shall be resubmitted for final review within seven (7) days of the receipt of feedback. Interim and final monitoring report shall not be deemed complete and acceptable until UNRWA has provided written approval confirming that all required factual revisions have been satisfactorily addressed. Draft reports and any related correspondence or feedback from UNRWA shall be treated as confidential and shall not be disclosed to any third party without the express written consent of the Agency.
* **Reporting to the Programme Board**: On the basis of cleared reports, the TPM contractor shall participate in Programme Board meetings, in-person or online, to report on monitoring results.

## Operational framework/ Roles and Responsibilities

1. Contractual arrangements with the TPM service provider will be managed by the UNRWA External Relations and Communication Department (ERCD). The TPM work will be conducted by an independent service provider. The team will report to the assigned focal point in the UNRWA Department of Planning (DoP) who will provide all documents and information required by the TPM service provider, facilitate access to staff and visits to UNRWA offices and organize meetings/interviews with relevant UNRWA staff. TPM deliverables will be quality-assured by UNRWA, as coordinated by the DoP focal point. The quality assurance process will not interfere with the views and independence of the TPM but will ensure that the findings are factually correct and based on evidence.
2. All UNRWA cooperation with the present TPM effort is subject to the Agency's regulatory framework, including in relation to confidentiality and information sharing, as well as safety and security restrictions.
3. The Programme Board shall provide guidance to the TPM service provider at key stages of the exercise. Endorsement by the Board will be required on the monitoring reports and the final report.

The TPM service provider shall bear sole responsibility for all costs, expenses and liabilities incurred that are outside the scope of the terms and conditions set forth in the contract agreed with UNRWA. Any such costs, unless expressly pre-approved in writing by UNRWA, will not be reimbursed or borne by the Agency. Furthermore, the service provider shall ensure that all expenditure related to the performance of the contract strictly adheres to the agreed budget and scope.

## TPM SOPs and Rules of Engagement

In relation to the TPM process that will be implemented during the period June 2026 through March 2027, the present section of these terms of reference defines TPM service provider and UNRWA responsibilities and to establish rules of engagement to ensure adherence to humanitarian principles and the independence of the TPM service provider and overall monitoring process.

For its part, UNRWA commits to:

1. Fully respect the independence of the TPM service provider and monitoring processes.
2. Convey to its staff and stakeholders the need for such independence and freedom of the TPM service provider to conduct monitoring processes in accordance with the present terms of reference.
3. Fully participate in and support the design of the monitoring effort, including planning meetings and interviews, as required.
4. Subject to the present terms of reference, and UNRWA's internal control/regulatory framework and confidentiality safeguards, provide the TPM service provider with all requested and relevant project / emergency intervention documentation in a timely manner to facilitate the review of these documents and field monitoring visits.
5. Facilitate TPM service provider access to relevant project / emergency intervention sites and key stakeholders, including, where relevant, the facilitation of permissions.

The TPM service provider commits to:

1. Always perform data collection in a professional and gender/disability/conflict-sensitive and courteous manner that respects the UNRWA internal control/regulatory framework.

![](data:image/png;base64...)

1. Ensure the confidentiality of all documents shared with the TPM service provider and to not disseminate this information, with the exception of documents that are in the public domain.
2. Always adhere to humanitarian principles (humanity, neutrality, impartiality and independence), bearing in mind the interests of the people serving in or served by the projects / emergency interventions under focus.
3. Abide by agreed arrangements with UNRWA for field level data collection, representation, interviews, photo taking, data/information validation or any other aspect of the TPM.
4. Where needed, (e.g., to interview beneficiaries etc.) arrange (at the cost of the TPM service provider) an Arabic translator.
5. Report findings in a fair and professional manner within the agreed timeline after the completion of monitoring visits.
6. Respect the privacy and need for anonymity of project / emergency intervention beneficiaries.
7. Report any inappropriate behavior observed or reported in the field(s) of focus, including any safeguarding issues, such as child abuse, sexual exploitation and abuse, sexual harassment, aggressive behavior, corruption, theft or aid diversion.

Prospective TPM service provider proposals should include a commitment to the present SOPs and rules of engagement. Complaints arising from the TPM process will be handled by the UNRWA ERCD focal point, liaising with UNRWA Field Offices, HQ Departments/Divisions and the TPM service provider, as appropriate.

## TPM team requirements

The TPM service provider should have at least two personnel who meet the following education, experience, and skill requirements. Proficiency in Japanese language in either of the team members is desirable.

**TPM service provider (Company)**

* Accredited or legally registered to provide consultancy services.
* Minimum of ten years of professional experience in monitoring and evaluation, including TPM, of humanitarian and/or development programmes.

**Team Leader:**

* **Education**: Master's degree from an accredited institution, preferably with a specialization in monitoring and evaluation or related disciplines.
* **Experience**: Minimum ten years’ experience in monitoring and evaluation, including developing monitoring frameworks, study design, and data analysis across multiple sectors.
* **Skills**: Experience in TPM, knowledge management software or databases, and working in the Middle East is highly desirable. Excellent communication, analysis and writing skills, in English, is required.

**Team Member:**

* **Education**: Master's degree from an accredited institution, preferably in monitoring and evaluation, economics, finance or related disciplines.
* **Experience**: Minimum seven years’ experience in reviewing and monitoring humanitarian and/or international development projects. Additional experience in developing monitoring frameworks or similar tools for project monitoring is required.
* **Skills**: Experience in TPM, knowledge management software or databases and working in the Middle East is highly desirable. Excellent communication, analysis and writing skills, in English, is required.

* Proposals will be evaluated using a combined scoring method where:
  + **Technical criteria**: Will be weighted at **70%** and will include a review of education, experience, language skills and the quality of the proposed TPM methodology.

* Only candidate TPM service providers that achieve a minimum of **60 points out of 100** from the technical review will be considered technically compliant and their financial bids reviewed.
* The contractor with the highest combined score, who accepts UNRWA’s general terms and conditions, will be awarded the contract.
