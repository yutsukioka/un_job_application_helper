# Selection policy

## Stable facts; application-specific presentation

Historical identity and evidence do not change with the vacancy. A role can
have more than one substantial, truthful facet. A vacancy-specific presentation
may emphasize an eligible facet only within the actual field's semantics.

Keep these independent:
- confirmed employment facts and source claims;
- a whole-role baseline classification;
- other evidence-supported substantive facets;
- verified portal options and their definitions;
- the area proposed/selected for this application;
- vacancy-specific narrative emphasis and skill selections.

Use evidence -> allowable facets -> vacancy relevance -> narrative. Never use
rewritten narrative -> new evidence -> new classification. Repeated application
optimization is reevaluation, not compulsory switching of the category.

For single primary-function fields, a minor task cannot displace the main
function simply because the new vacancy values it. For a genuinely integrated
role, a broad sector category can be accurate if the enum definition permits it.
Be explicit when only a category name, rather than its help-text definition,
has been observed. UNKNOWN is not permission to assume an accommodating meaning.

## Field limits and scope

Requested shortlist budget: 20 by default, an adapter drafting default, overridable by the user.
Verified portal limit: unknown until observed. Record its source and scope:
PROFILE, APPLICATION, PER_ROLE, or UNKNOWN. Do not apply a per-role limit globally
or multiply a global limit by the number of roles. Existing profile selections
consume capacity when the actual field is shared; prepare a diff rather than
silently deleting/replacing selections. Do not test limits by submitting.

Online persistence: UNKNOWN by default. Store application-specific local
snapshots and do not alter a shared profile without explicit approval and an
assessment of whether previous applications would be affected.

## Evidence gates

SUPPORTED means supported by the approved source inputs, not independently
verified with a former employer. Distinguish original performance evidence,
approved factual assertions, raw history, and earlier generated prose.

No scoring formula may overcome missing proof or a requirement not met. Score
only after catalog/meaning/evidence gates. Prefer lexicographic decisions to
an arbitrary weighted score: required-criterion coverage first, then quality and
scope of evidence, desirable-criterion coverage and distinct useful information.
No external UNOPS weighting or hiring probability is claimed.

Examples of rejected shortcuts:
- Selecting Health because the vacancy mentions health, when the old role only
  provided unrelated general support to a health-sector employer.
- Rejecting Health solely because an evidenced financing/UHC role was nonclinical.
- Selecting Project Finance for ordinary donor-budget administration.
- Selecting PMP certification because projects were managed.
- Inferring actuarial pricing from a CBHI feasibility study with no pricing work.
- Inferring machine learning, SQL or clinical care from broad dictionary examples.
- Awarding 36 months for a task observed once during a three-year appointment.
- Inferring personal fundraising credit from the entire grant portfolio managed.

## Reuse and audits

Recompute on changes to vacancy, accepted claims, catalog or portal constraints.
The source of truth is the fit plan; generators are views, not new fact sources.
Preserve prior application snapshots. Categorization changes need explanations,
not fabricated continuity. More specific, well-supported wording can improve
legibility but is not a guarantee of passing any undisclosed selection process.
